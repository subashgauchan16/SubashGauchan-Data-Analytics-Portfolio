01 · What happened to the orders

Only 19% of orders were delivered

The workbook's pivot adds up every order, including cancelled, returned and pending ones. That figure is gross order value. Revenue that actually landed needs the delivered orders alone.

02 · Over time

Order value rises and falls without a clear trend

Monthly value swings between roughly 28k and 68k with no steady direction. Quarterly value falls from about 145k in 2023 Q1 to 104k in 2025 Q1, then is 128k in 2025 Q2. The data stops in June 2025.

04 · Channels, coupons and payment

The best channel depends on the question

Instagram brings the most order value. Email brings the most delivered value. One of the old dashboard notes gave the second answer as though it were the first.

One pattern worth checking. Email has the highest delivered share (24.0% of its orders) and Referral the lowest (14.9%). A chi-square test of channel against status gives p ≈ 0.005, the only one of six checks I ran that stood out, so it is a lead for further work and not a conclusion. Channel totals themselves are within what chance could produce.

05 · How it was built

From raw rows to a dashboard

Step 1Raw data1,200 orders from January 2023 to June 2025 with product, quantity, price, status, coupon, referral source and payment method.
Step 2ChecksNo duplicate order IDs. Total price equals quantity × unit price on every row. A stray total row was removed. 309 orders have no coupon code and are counted as "no coupon".
Step 3PivotsPivot tables summarise value by product, quarter and referral source, with slicers on the Excel dashboard.
Step 4This dashboardThe same rows now drive every chart here, so the filters above always agree with each other.
