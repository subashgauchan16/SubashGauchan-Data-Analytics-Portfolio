Task 1 · The supply network

Where the plan and the reality part ways

Select a stage of the network. The plan describes the intention, the reality is what the case shows, and the barrier is why the two differ.

Why the usual input-transformation-output view is not enough. It shows what enters and leaves the factory but not why flow becomes unstable. At Green Motion, demand information enters before any material is bought, so a weak forecast destabilises everything after it.

Task 2 · Inventory and capacity

Having more stock is not the same as having the right stock

Frames pile up while controllers and sensors run out, so the line stops anyway. The report classifies this as misalignment with the production constraint, not a shortage of stock overall.

Task 2 · TIMWOODS

Eight wastes, one shared root

Tap a card to see the operational consequence. The report's main analysis centres on inventory, waiting and overproduction, and the appendix covers all eight.

Reading across the cards. Several wastes come from the same instability. Weak forecasting feeds overproduction and excess inventory, and shortages create waiting. So waste reduction should start with the planning process, not with each waste on its own.

Task 3 and 4 · Benchmark and PDCA

From a Lime benchmark to three recommendations

Select a phase, or click the plate, and the ring turns to it.

Plan in detail: Green Motion against Lime

Lime is a digital benchmark. Its shared-micromobility model differs from manufacturing, so the lesson is its information integration and data-led allocation. The report does not claim Lime uses centralised procurement.

Proof, not assumption

How an improvement would actually be shown

PDCA, the 4Vs and TIMWOODS structure an investigation, but a framework alone does not demonstrate an improvement. This is the loop the analysis holds itself to, and the measures that would settle it.

    Yes, performance improvedStandardise the change and monitor it, then continue the improvement cycle.
    No, it did notReview the assumptions, revise the intervention and return to step 02 to measure again.

    Measures that would decide it

    Which measures apply depends on the data available. Forecast error is usually expressed as MAPE, mean absolute percentage error.

    What is not claimed. This coursework reports no measured productivity improvement. The 40% growth plan, the stock positions and the Lime comparison are analytical inputs and illustrations, not results.

    Verification and limits

    What this analysis does and does not show

    A fictional case. Green Motion is a course case, so the inventory classification and the Brompton comparison are hypothetical. Brompton is a real comparator, and the report does not claim it has the same failures. The analysis proposes measures and reports no measured improvement.
