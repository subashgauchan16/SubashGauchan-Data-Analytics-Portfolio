Can a Ridge regression beat a rolling-average baseline at forecasting the 20-day price change? Tested year by year, 2015 to 2026, on seven markets. The answer so far: not reliably.

04 · Open reconciliation items

The headline and the yearly table do not yet agree

A good review checks its own numbers. The headline directional accuracy puts Ridge well behind the baseline. The simple average of the twelve yearly values puts them level.

MetricHeadline in the write-upAverage of the 12 yearly values
Three numbers, three conflicts. Direction: the write-up says Ridge wins in only two years, the yearly table shows four. Instruments: the review page and the README chart list different seven-instrument sets with different latest forecasts. Both agree on the conclusion that forecasts sit inside typical error.
Why it matters. The error figures line up, but the direction figures point to opposite conclusions. A pooled score can differ from a simple average of yearly scores, so this may be a weighting or definition issue. Until it is reconciled, neither direction figure should carry the argument.

05 · Latest forecast, 14 October 2026 close

Every forecast sits inside the typical error

The marker is the forecast change over the next 20 days. The shaded band is the model's typical error for that instrument. Where the marker stays well inside the band, the forecast is not a usable call on its own.

BP and Shell prices are in pence on the London Stock Exchange. The other five are index points. BP prices look dividend-adjusted and may be more volatile. Errors are in percentage points, from all test years.

What this does not show

Status. Exploratory prototype. The evaluation does not demonstrate a reliable predictive edge or investment profitability, and nothing here is investment advice.
